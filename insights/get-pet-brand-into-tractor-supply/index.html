How to get your pet brand into Tractor Supply

Ahmed RehmanEx-Nestlé Purina, Hill's, Moose Toys · Updated November 25, 2026
Short answer

Tractor Supply takes new product proposals through RangeMe, and buyers review them as their category reviews come up. Get in by showing you fit its rural, practical shopper: real value per pound or per dose, packs sized for big dogs and multi-pet homes, and proof the product already sells somewhere. Then plan how you'll drive shoppers to the shelf, because a rural store won't do that for you.

Why Tractor Supply matters for pet brands

Tractor Supply is one of the biggest pet retailers in the US that most founders forget to plan for. It has more than 2,000 stores, most of them in small towns and rural areas where there may be no PetSmart or Petco nearby. Pet food and supplies are a core reason people visit, and its shoppers tend to own big dogs, several pets, and often livestock as well.

It also owns Petsense, a chain of smaller pet specialty stores in small towns, and Allivet, an online pet pharmacy. For a pet brand, that is a lot of reach in places your competitors often ignore.

How to submit your product

Tractor Supply asks new suppliers to submit products through RangeMe, a free product discovery platform. You build a listing with your product details, pricing, images and sales history, then submit it to Tractor Supply's merchandising team. Buyers look at submissions when their category reviews come up or when they have a gap to fill, so a good listing can sit for a while before anyone acts on it.

A listing on its own rarely wins the slot. What moves a buyer is a clear story: which shopper you serve, why your product beats what is already on the shelf, and the proof that people buy it again. If you are approved, you will sign a vendor agreement and get set up with a vendor number before your first order.

Tractor Supply has run Open Buying Days in the past, where suppliers pitched buyers in person. Check its Vendor Center for current events before you plan around one.

What Tractor Supply buyers look for

  • Fit with a rural, practical shopper. Products that solve real problems for working dogs, big breeds, farm cats and multi-pet homes do better than premium lifestyle stories.
  • Value they can see. Price per pound, per serving or per dose matters. Larger pack sizes often make sense here when they would not at a boutique.
  • Proof it sells. Sales velocity at other retailers, online reviews and repeat rates. Buyers want to know it will move without them pushing it.
  • A margin that works for them after your trade spend, and a promotional plan that does not rely on constant deep discounts.
  • Reliable supply. A rural chain cannot fix an out of stock quickly, so they need confidence you will ship on time and in full.

Tractor Supply also sells its own pet food brands, such as 4health and Retriever. Know where you sit against them on price and quality, and be ready to explain why a shopper would pay more for yours.

Price for the Tractor Supply shopper

Many brands walk in with the same price ladder they use at pet specialty stores. That can work for supplements and specialist products, but for food and treats you often need a pack and price built for this shopper. Work out your landed cost, the retailer's margin and your trade spend first, then design the pack. It is much easier to launch at the right price than to fix it after the first line review.

Keep the shelf moving

Getting listed is the start. Rural stores have less foot traffic than a big city superstore, so your first 90 days need a plan to send shoppers in: local and social ads near the stores, sampling, retailer media where it pays back, and a reason to come back, such as offers through Tractor Supply's Neighbor's Club loyalty program. Track sales by store every week and fix the slow ones before the next review. It's the same approach that helped FortiFlora become #1 in vet and retail.

Common questions

Does it cost money to submit to Tractor Supply on RangeMe?

No. Tractor Supply says registering and submitting on RangeMe is free, and you do not need a paid RangeMe package to be considered.

How long does Tractor Supply take to review a product?

It depends on the category review calendar. Buyers review submissions when their category comes up or when they need something new, so it can take months. A broker or direct buyer contact can help you time it.

Is Tractor Supply a good fit for premium pet food?

It can be, if you can explain the value to a practical shopper and price it fairly. Brands that lean only on lifestyle stories usually struggle there.
