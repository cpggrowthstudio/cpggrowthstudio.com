Selling pet products at Target and Costco

Ahmed RehmanEx-Nestlé Purina, Hill's, Moose Toys · Updated November 25, 2026
Short answer

Target and Costco both sell a lot of pet products but buy very differently. Target wants on-trend brands that fit its style, a clean price ladder and a plan to drive guests in, and you start at its supplier pages. Costco carries few items and buys on value: one strong item, a big pack and the lowest cost you offer anyone, usually through a buyer conversation rather than an open portal. Pick the one that fits your brand, not both at once.

Target: style, trend and the guest

Target's pet aisle mixes national brands with its own, such as Boots & Barkley. Target shoppers, which it calls guests, come for a curated, good-looking assortment at a fair price. Brands that win there usually have a clear design, a trend story (natural, functional, fun) and enough buzz online to bring guests in.

To start, look at the supplier information on Target's corporate site. Target also runs Target Plus, a curated online marketplace where approved partners sell and ship directly to Target.com shoppers. For some brands that's a way to build a sales record with Target before going after store shelves.

What Target buyers look for

  • A brand that looks like it belongs at Target, on shelf and online.
  • Sales and social proof: velocity at other retailers, strong reviews, a following.
  • A price ladder that sits sensibly against the national brands and Target's own.
  • Marketing support, including retailer media through Roundel, Target's media network, social and creators.
  • Supply chain readiness for a national retailer with strict shipping and labeling rules.

Costco: one item, big value

Costco works the other way. It carries a small number of items in each category and turns them fast. Its own Kirkland Signature pet food is a big seller, so a branded item has to give members a clear reason to pay more. Costco keeps its own markup low, so it expects the best cost you give any retailer.

There is no open application portal. Most brands start with Costco's vendor inquiry channel, a broker who already sells to Costco, or a regional buyer. Many items start in one region, sometimes through in-club demos or roadshows, and expand if they sell.

What Costco buyers look for

  • A value pack. Bigger sizes or multi-packs that give members a clear deal per unit.
  • Pallet-ready packaging that sells itself without a store associate, since there's little shelf help in a warehouse club.
  • A strong cost. Your best price, with room to fund demos.
  • Food safety and audits. Food and treat makers should expect a supplier audit.
  • Proof. Strong sales elsewhere, especially online, where Costco.com is sometimes a first step.

Which one first?

If your brand is about design, trend and a premium feel, Target is usually the better fit. If you have a hero item with great value in a big pack and the margin to fund demos, Costco can move huge volume. Most brands should not chase both at once, because the packs, prices and marketing are different. If you're still choosing between retailers, start with how buyers decide at PetSmart, Petco and Walmart.

Common questions

Can a small pet brand get into Costco?

Yes, but usually with one strong item, a value pack and a cost that beats what other retailers get. Many start in a single region and grow from there.

Does Target have an online-only option for pet brands?

Yes. Target Plus is Target's curated online marketplace, where approved partners sell and ship directly to Target.com shoppers.

Should I sell at Target and Costco at the same time?

Usually not at first. They need different packs and prices, and a value pack at Costco can undercut your price at Target.
