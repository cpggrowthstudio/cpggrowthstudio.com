Pet food broker vs consultant: which do you need?

Ahmed RehmanEx-Nestlé Purina, Hill's, Moose Toys · Updated November 25, 2026
Short answer

A broker sells your product to retail buyers and looks after it in stores, usually for a commission on sales. A consultant or fractional CMO builds the plan that makes the product easy to sell: the pitch, pricing, launch and sell-through. Brokers do best with brands that are already retail-ready. If you're not there yet, you need the plan first, and most growing brands end up needing both.

What a broker does

A broker represents your brand to retail buyers they already know. Good brokers get you meetings, present your items at line reviews, handle paperwork and promotions with the retailer, and in many cases check on your products in stores. Their value is their relationships and their time in front of buyers.

Brokers are usually paid a commission on your sales at the retailers they cover, often a few percent of net sales. Some ask for a monthly retainer from new brands, because it takes time before those sales arrive.

What a consultant or fractional CMO does

A consultant, or a fractional CMO, works on the brand side. They help you decide which retailers to go after, build the pitch and the proof, set a price ladder and trade budget that works, plan the launch, and run the marketing that drives sell-through once you're on shelf. Their value is the plan and the follow-through. They're usually paid a monthly fee or a project fee. You can see what that typically costs in what a fractional CMO costs.

Are you ready for a broker?

Brokers spend their time where sales come fastest. Before you sign one, check that you have:

  • Pricing that leaves room for the retailer's margin, the broker's commission and trade spend.
  • Sales proof from somewhere, even if it's online or a regional chain.
  • Supply that can handle a chain-wide order.
  • A marketing plan and budget to drive shoppers once you're listed.

If any of these is missing, fix it first. A broker can't sell a price that doesn't work.

Common questions

How much does a pet food broker charge?

Most work on commission, often a few percent of net sales at the retailers they cover. Some ask new brands for a monthly retainer as well.

Can I sell to retailers without a broker?

Yes. Many retailers, including Petco and Tractor Supply, take submissions directly through RangeMe. A broker mainly helps with timing, relationships and in-store follow-through.

Do I need a fractional CMO if I have a broker?

If your broker is getting you meetings but products aren't selling through, yes. The broker sells it in. The plan behind it has to sell it out.
